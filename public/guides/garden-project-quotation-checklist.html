What should your garden-project quotation include?

A clear quotation helps you understand the work, compare options and agree what happens next. Use these questions when planning a garden room, decking or a combined outdoor project.

Discuss your project ↗

Published 28 September 2026 · Tom Cutts Joinery & Building

1. A description of the agreed work

Start with the purpose of the project, its position and approximate dimensions. Ask for the main materials and finishes to be named. For a garden room, that might include cladding, glazing, internal finish and the services being considered. For decking, it includes the board specification, layout, edges and steps.

Photographs and a simple marked-up sketch can help explain the discussion. If drawings or specialist design are needed, establish who provides them and how they become part of the agreed scope.

2. Site preparation and access

Discuss what is already in the space and what must happen before construction. Existing structures, changes in ground level and the access route all need to be understood. Confirm what the quotation includes for preparation, deliveries, storage of materials, protection of surrounding areas and disposal of waste.

3. Inclusions, exclusions and choices

Make it easy to see which items are included, which are optional and which sit outside the quotation. A room price may need to be considered alongside specialist services, decoration, flooring or work to the surrounding garden. Ask whether applicable VAT is included and whether any amount is an allowance that still needs a final selection.

If you are combining a room and a deck, ask for the relationship between them to be clear. This helps you compare a complete project with quotations that cover only one part.

4. Responsibilities and checks

Agree who will coordinate the job and who handles any specialist work, permissions enquiries or design checks that the project requires. Ask what information you need to supply and which decisions must be made before a start can be arranged. The answer should relate to your site and planned use.

5. Timing and changes

Discuss the proposed programme, the factors it depends on and how progress will be communicated. If you change a material, add work or choose a different layout, ask how the revised scope, cost and timing will be recorded before that work goes ahead.

A clear written change avoids two different understandings of what was agreed. Keep the original quotation, selections and later amendments together.

6. Payment and handover

Ask for the payment stages and their associated milestones to be explained before agreeing to proceed. At handover, confirm what care information, product paperwork, specialist documentation or workmanship terms apply to your particular project. Keep a note of how to raise any outstanding questions.
