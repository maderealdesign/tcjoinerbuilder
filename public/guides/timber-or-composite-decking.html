Timber or composite decking?

The right choice depends on the look you want, the particular product, your garden and the care you are happy to give it. Compare a complete deck specification, rather than a board price alone.

Discuss your project ↗

Published 28 September 2026 · Tom Cutts Joinery & Building

Choose the appearance you want to live with

Timber offers natural grain and variation. The species, treatment and finish affect its appearance and suitability outdoors. The Timber Decking & Cladding Association explains that the words hardwood and softwood describe groups of trees; they are not a simple ranking of how durable a board will be. Read the TDCA timber guidance.

Composite boards offer manufactured finishes in a range of colours and profiles. Products differ, so look at samples of the actual range being quoted. Consider them beside your brickwork, garden room and paving, rather than choosing from a small picture alone.

Understand the care involved

Both types need cleaning. Leaves, dirt and surface growth can affect a deck, particularly in shaded or damp areas. The TDCA recommends regular cleaning for timber and wood-plastic composite surfaces; it also notes that decorative timber coatings need refreshing to maintain their appearance. Follow the care instructions for the specific board and finish. See the TDCA maintenance advice.

Some composite ranges avoid routine painting or staining. That does not mean every product is maintenance-free. Ask which cleaning products are suitable, what could damage the surface and what to do if an individual board needs attention.

Check claims against the actual product

Do not assume all composite boards perform alike. Formulations and designs vary, and the supporting structure and installation must suit the chosen system. Ask to see its installation instructions, care guidance and written warranty. The TDCA explains why product-specific evidence matters.

Think about the conditions in your garden. Manufacturer Trex, for example, says its decking can become slick in wet or icy weather and that its boards can get hot in direct sun. Those are reasons to discuss exposure and use, rather than accept a blanket claim of a surface being slip-proof or always cool. See Trex’s product FAQs. This is an example of manufacturer guidance, not a claim that a particular brand is included in your quotation.

Compare the cost of the finished deck

The board is only one part of the job. Ask each quotation to identify the area, preparation, supporting frame, boards, fixings, edges, steps and any guarding or additional work. The layout and access can affect the installation as well as the material choice.

A sensible comparison uses the same dimensions and scope. If one option costs more, ask exactly what changes in the product, finish or construction. Avoid choosing on a headline price that leaves essential parts of the job unlisted.

Five questions to bring to our team

  • Which material and finish suit the look of my house and garden?
  • What care does this exact board need?
  • How does the design deal with the site, levels and access?
  • What is included in the complete installation price?
  • What written product and workmanship information will I receive?

Send your postcode, approximate dimensions and photographs of the space. Include how you want to use the deck: dining, seating, access to a garden room or another purpose. Our team can then discuss options around the actual project.
